# FORM Athletic Club — business blueprint

This is an original proposed business, not an existing gym. All pricing, volumes, costs and operating targets below are planning assumptions to validate against an actual city, premises, staffing model and demand. USD is a display currency, not an assumption about location.

## Positioning and audience

Promise: purposeful training that becomes an everyday habit. Brand line: Built for your kind of strong. Voice: calm, direct, encouraging, without body shaming or transformation guarantees. Visual language: warm ivory, charcoal, electric lime, generous spacing, expressive serif accents and abstract athlete artwork.

Primary audience: adults seeking a sustainable routine, including first-time gym members and busy professionals. Secondary audience: regular trainees seeking coaching and progression. The digital planner is limited to adults; youth programming would require a separate professionally designed service.

## Class portfolio and delivery

Strength Lab: 50-minute session with warm-up, progressive movement patterns and technique coaching. Proposed cap: 12.

Engine Room: 40-minute adaptable conditioning session using bikes, walking, rowing and intervals selected by a coach. Proposed cap: 14.

Restore & Flow: 45-minute mobility and balance session. Proposed cap: 16.

Initial concept: 24 classes per week across morning, lunchtime and evening windows. At an average 14 seats, this yields 336 weekly class places. The demo timetable is a smaller illustrative set, not the production capacity schedule. Track attendance, waitlists and peak-hour utilization before expanding unlimited-class memberships.

If 200 Plus members average 2 classes/week, their demand alone is 400 places/week, above this starting supply. Add sessions, limit membership sales, or adjust the product before reaching that volume. Unlimited classes still require reservations and available capacity, with these limits disclosed.

## Member journey

1. Discover the brand, coaches, classes and pricing.
2. Create a starting schedule or request an orientation.
3. Complete account creation, consent and a coach-reviewed intake.
4. Coach checks experience, preferences, accessibility, relevant limitations and readiness; assigns the first program.
5. Select a subscription, pay through a hosted payment page and book available classes.
6. Receive a realistic weekly schedule with reminders, rest days and substitutions.
7. Log attendance, perceived effort and optional progress measures.
8. Review after two weeks, then monthly; adjust using actual experience and coach judgment.

## Personalization principles

The working demo adjusts session type for the goal, weekly allocation for attendance, set count for experience, and uses conservative pacing for older adults. Gender, height and weight do not infer ability or define an ideal body. Measurement inputs are context only; future collection should be optional and justified by a real coaching purpose.

Production personalization should also consider preferred days, equipment, accessibility, injuries, training history, sleep/recovery preferences and coach-approved restrictions. Do not automatically diagnose, prescribe rehabilitation, or create dietary prescriptions. Members should be able to decline unnecessary measurements and still access a program. Explain why every requested field is collected.

Progression: review attendance, technique and member feedback before changing volume. Provide alternatives and a way to flag discomfort. Coaching approval precedes the publication of personalized programs. Avoid unsupported claims of precision from body measurements or automated rules.

## Illustrative unit economics

Example member mix: 100 Club, 200 Plus, 50 Coached.

Monthly recurring revenue at monthly list prices: $4,900 + $17,800 + $8,950 = **$31,650**, or **$90.43 average revenue per member**. Annual discounts reduce recognized monthly revenue; annual cash receipts must not be confused with monthly revenue.

Assume $23,000/month fixed operating cost and $12/member variable cost: contribution per member is $78.43, with approximate break-even at **294 members** under that mix. At 350 members, modeled operating contribution after those assumed costs is **$4,450/month**, before taxes, financing, startup expenses and owner distributions. These figures are not forecasts; replace every assumption with local quotes and measured demand.

Coached capacity: 50 members × 4 sessions = 200 personal sessions/month. At 45 minutes plus 15 minutes turnover, allocate 200 staff hours/month. At an assumed $20 loaded cost per hour, direct PT labor is $4,000/month. If this is not already included in fixed staffing cost, the illustrative $4,450 contribution falls to $450. Prevent double counting and verify whether the $179 tier covers coaching, overhead and desired margin. At a higher labor cost, raise price or reduce included sessions before selling the tier.

Use a capacity and staffing model alongside financial calculations. Gym equipment, flooring, fit-out, deposit and working capital are separate startup costs. Obtain quotes rather than assuming a universal setup budget.

## Acquisition and retention

Prelaunch: local interest page, founding-member orientation events, nearby business partnerships and coach introductions. Opening: scheduled visits and small beginner cohorts. Ongoing: useful technique content, member referral credits and corporate discovery sessions.

Proposed funnel metrics: visitor → interest → orientation → paid membership; acquisition cost by channel; 30/90-day retention; class attendance; contribution margin; no-show rate; coaching hours per member; cancellation reasons. Avoid paid acquisition until orientation conversion and retention are measurable.

## Operations and ownership

Club manager owns capacity, scheduling, service issues and commercial policies. Lead coach owns program review and staff development. Coaches own session delivery and member feedback. Admin owns subscription support, reconciliation and consent records. Keep permission boundaries between admin financial functions and sensitive coaching notes.

Launch decision gates: premises and staffing ready; verified real coach identities; final subscription terms; sufficient capacity; payment reconciliation tested; coach-reviewed intake; privacy and deletion paths implemented; support contact and operational timetable confirmed.
